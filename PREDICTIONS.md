# Predictions & Falsifiability

This document outlines specific, testable predictions derived from the Emergent Hypergraph Physics (EHP) model. If these predictions are contradicted by empirical data, the model must be refined or rejected.

## 1. Gravitational Time Dilation Discrepancy
Standard General Relativity predicts time dilation based on spacetime curvature. EHP predicts that time dilation is fundamentally an algorithmic artifact of increased node density.
* **Prediction:** In extremely high-density environments (near black hole horizons), EHP expects a "quantization effect" in time measurement.
* **Test:** Analyze high-precision pulsar timing data for deviations from GR that correlate with discrete node-hopping intervals.

## 2. Cosmic Expansion as Density Relaxation
EHP views the expansion of the universe as the relaxation of node density (the hypergraph "spreading out").
* **Prediction:** The expansion rate is not governed by a mysterious "Dark Energy" constant but by the internal rewriting rules of the hypergraph trying to reach a minimal-energy state.
* **Test:** The expansion rate should show "step-like" behavior at extreme scales, rather than a perfectly smooth continuous curve. If observations show smooth acceleration, the current rewriting rule needs adjustment.

## 3. Limits of Matter Density
Since the hypergraph has a finite node-rewriting speed, there should be a maximum possible density for any cluster before the rewriting rule fails to compute the state.
* **Prediction:** There is a theoretical limit to the mass-density of an object (a "saturated cluster"). Beyond this point, the object must either collapse into a new topological state or shed information.
* **Test:** Compare predicted maximum densities with observed neutron star core densities.

## 4. Anisotropy in Light Propagation
If space is a discrete lattice, there should be a preferred direction for information propagation based on the hypergraph's intrinsic structure.
* **Prediction:** Light speed might show infinitesimal variations depending on the orientation of the path relative to the underlying "base lattice" of the hypergraph.
* **Test:** Extremely long-baseline interferometry seeking direction-dependent discrepancies in the speed of light.

## 5. Algorithmic "Age" vs. Observed Distance

* **Prediction:** The "age" of the universe is not 13.8 billion years, but the total number of ticks. 
* **Test:** Using the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), we can calculate the total "information content" of the early universe. If EHP is correct, the ratio of the total node-ticks should match the observed complexity of the CMB patterns.

## 6. Falsification Criteria
The model is considered falsified if:
1. Continuous, non-discrete values are proven to be fundamental and not emergent.
2. Experiments detect phenomena that require non-integer dimensions that cannot be mapped to a hypergraph.
3. The speed of light is proven to be strictly independent of node-density in environments where EHP predicts a measurable Shapiro-like delay.
