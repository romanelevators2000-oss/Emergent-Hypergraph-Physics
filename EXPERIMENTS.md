# Experimental Suite: Validating Discrete Dynamics

This document describes the primary experiments designed to validate the Emergent Hypergraph Physics (EHP) model against empirical physical observations.

## 1. Experiment: Shapiro Delay Replication
* **Goal:** Confirm that local node density creates a macroscopic time delay.
* **Setup:** Create a hypergraph of 10^6 nodes. Define a "Mass Cluster" at the center with a density factor 10x higher than the background.
* **Procedure:** 1. Fire a "Photon" (a signal pulse) from point A to point B.
    2. Measure the number of computational ticks required to cross the cluster vs. a path of equal distance through a vacuum (uniform density).
* **Expected Result:** A measurable time lag proportional to the mass of the cluster, matching the general relativity constant for the Shapiro delay.

## 2. Experiment: Entropy Emergence
* **Goal:** Determine if irreversibility emerges from deterministic rewriting rules.
* **Setup:** Initialize a high-order symmetric crystal lattice (low entropy state).
* **Procedure:** 1. Apply the transformation rule $T$ repeatedly.
    2. Monitor the "Graph Information Complexity" (the number of unique node connections).
* **Expected Result:** A spontaneous transition from ordered structure to a state of maximum connectivity (high information entropy), demonstrating that thermodynamic arrow of time is a statistical property of the hypergraph.

## 3. Experiment: Fractal Cluster Stability
* **Goal:** Verify that matter structures are self-organizing.
* **Setup:** Randomly distribute nodes and apply the local density-growth rule.
* **Procedure:**
    1. Allow the system to iterate through 100,000 ticks.
    2. Cluster analysis: Measure the geometric properties of high-density areas.
* **Expected Result:** The emergence of stable, fractal-like polyhedral structures that persist over time, confirming that particles are "stable solitons" of the hypergraph.

## 4. Experiment: Propagation Anisotropy
* **Goal:** Check for "lattice bias" (directional dependence).
* **Setup:** A large-scale hypergraph simulation.
* **Procedure:** 1. Send multiple signals in 360 degrees from a central source.
    2. Compare the number of ticks required to reach a specific distance in different directions.
* **Expected Result:** Identification of the "Planck-scale noise floor." If the results show strong anisotropy, the rewriting rules must be updated to include rotational randomization.

## 5. Experiment: Redshift Simulation
* **Goal:** Link algorithmic time to cosmological expansion.
* **Setup:** Simulate a expanding "Universe" starting from maximum node density.
* **Procedure:** 1. Track the "ticks per distance" ratio over the life of the simulation.
    2. Map the tick-density drop to the observed Cosmic Microwave Background redshift.
* **Expected Result:** Convergence to a universe age of ~46 billion light-years (conformal time).
